3D Clinostat For Simulated Microgravity in Cell Cultures

Abdel Yasser, Rylee Rolstone, Sasha Ramchandani, Scott Garneau

Introduction

A 3D clinostat simulates microgravity by continuously rotating a sample around two perpendicular axes. By constantly altering the orientation relative to Earth, the direction of gravity changes faster than cells can perceive or adapt.

[Image Placeholder: Perpendicular Axes Diagram]

This cancels out the directional force of gravity over time, resulting in a time-averaged gravitational vector near zero.

Problem Statement

Commercial 3D clinostats are expensive and often too large for standard cell culture incubators. There is a need for a compact, modular system that keeps electronic controls outside the incubator while allowing the motorized rotating assembly to operate inside under controlled temperature and CO2 conditions.

Design Objectives

  • Incubator Compatibility: Build a compact assembly with biocompatible, heat-resistant materials designed to operate within humid cell culture incubator conditions.
  • Split Architecture: Separate the internal motorized rotating frame from external controller and power electronics using sealed cables.
  • Custom Sample Handling: Implement a removable rack system to securely hold multiple tissue culture chambers and flasks.
  • Dual-Axis Control: Enable independent low-speed rotation (0.5-5rpm) across two perpendicular axes for precise microgravity simulation.

Design & Analysis

Existing 3D clinostat designs rely on two main approaches:

  • Base-Mounted Motors (Belt/Gear Drive): Keeps motors stationary to reduce rotating mass and simplify wiring. However, added mechanical complexity increases maintenance and risks positioning errors from backlash or belt slippage.
  • Frame-Mounted Motors (Direct Drive): Mounts motors on rotating frames, simplifying the mechanism and improving positioning accuracy. The trade-offs are increased outer-axis mass (requiring higher torque) and wiring challenges during continuous rotation.

We came up with a hybrid approach, employing a direct-mounted motor on our frame to simplify assembly, along with a base-mounted motor and pulley system to achieve a torque boost of 3:1.

Conclusions

  • Successful System Integration: Designed, built, and validated a compact, low-cost 3D clinostat tailored for continuous operation within standard cell culture incubators.
  • Dual-Axis Performance: Achieved smooth, independent rotational control (0.5-5rpm) across perpendicular axes while isolating controller electronics from high-humidity environments.
  • Research Accessibility: Delivers a scalable, open-hardware alternative to expensive commercial platforms for simulated microgravity biological research.

Future Work

  • Biological verification using g-level testing
  • Making the inner frame easier to detach, and designing inner frames for single bottle batches
  • Built-in charging for our accelerometer
  • Making the machine more compact
  • Aesthetic improvements and finding a larger 3D printer to print our base in a single piece
